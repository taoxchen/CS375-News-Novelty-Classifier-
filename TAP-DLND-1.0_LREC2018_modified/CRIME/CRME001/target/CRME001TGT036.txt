 
A Jodhpur court on Wednesday acquitted Bollywood actor Salman Khan of all charges in the Arms Act case against him in the alleged poaching of two black bucks. 
Salman had pleaded “not guilty” on charges of violating the law by possessing unlicensed weapons and using them while shooting Hum Saath Saath Hain in Jodhpur in 1998. The court acquitted him by giving him the benefit of doubt, NDTV reported.

The actor’s lawyer argued there was no proof of him carrying firearms, and that he was only carrying air guns. 
Chief judicial magistrate Dalpat Singh Rajpurohit had earlier reserved the judgement in the respective case. In July last year, the Rajasthan High Court acquitted Salman in the 18-year-old blackbuck and chinkara poaching cases. 
The actor had appealed before the Jodhpur bench of the high court challenging a lower court in two separate cases of poaching.

The hearing was completed in the last week of May and the order reserved by the high court. “He has been acquitted in both the cases by the high court,” Salman’s counsel Hastimal Saraswat said. He was accused of killing a blackbuck and chinkara in two separate incidents.
