CHENNAI: After its hope of getting a berth in the DMK-led alliance turning futile, the Tamil Maanila Congress (TMC) on Tuesday announced that the party will go it  alone in the civic polls.

A visibly distraught party president GK Vasan made the announcement, having waited for seven days for a positive signal from the DMK after his meeting with MK Stalin. “The preparatory works for facing the local body polls are being done well in all the districts,” he told reporters.

Replying to question on why the move to forge ties with DMK failed to fructify, he said the Dravidian party had made its stand over its alliance partners five days ago.

Sombre mood prevailed at the TMC headquarters in Teynampet here, with the morale of the leaders and cadre drooping since the DMK had left them stranded. Party leaders sounded philosophical and looked resigned to what they are destined to in the October 17, 19 polls.

“We have to go to the polls on our own strength. We will muster all our strength and try to win as many seats as we can in places where we have support,” said a TMC leader.

The Congress putting up stiff resistance to the entry of its splinter group is believed to have tilted the scales against TMC, as DMK chief M Karunanidhi vetoed his son and party treasurer MK Stalin who was keen to have TMC on board. It’s like beign caught in the crossfire, said party leaders.

The task is cut out for TMC as it has to toil a lot to win at least a few seats in the civic polls.

The predicament of the party now seems to be one resembling the Assembly polls. Then, the TMC had hoped to board the AIADMK front but the later's insistence of contesting on the two-leaves symbol came as roadblock. With DMK not showing interest, Vasan had no option but to seek the company of PWA-DMDK combine, eventually drawing a blank.

“We constituted a viable alliance and faced the Assembly polls with a view to establish a polite, people’s welfare oriented alternative in the State. It had better response in the beginning. Lack of enough time to take our ideals to the people left us losing,” Vasan explained.
