NEW DELHI: The Congress has taken a hardline stand on Pakistan, demanding that the Modi government convene a special session of Parliament to declare Pakistan a terror state. The other specific Congress demands include imposition of economic sanctions against Pakistan, scrapping of MFN status, scaling down of strength in the two high commissions and immediate grant of political asylum to Baloch leader Brahamdagh Bugti . Congress also favoured a "calibrated" action on the Indus Waters Treaty.The nature of the Congress demands, publicised after a meeting of senior leaders, appears to be designed to tap the post-Uri public anger and to project the Modi government as an indecisive one that needs to be pressured and guided to act firmly. The main Opposition also seems to be trying to be seen one step ahead of the Modi government to demonstrate its stand against Pakistan.Making the demands at the AICC briefing, spokesman Abhishek Singhvi said: "For 9 days since Uri attack, the Modi government has been silent and no accountability has been fixed. The PM's speech demonstrates strategic ambiguity. We don't want this to lead to strategic confusion, making India a laughing stock. Congress believes in strategic restrain. But strategic restrain, in the face of such provocation, should not mean the absence of a strategic response." Maintaining that "extreme situation demands extreme decisions", Singhvi reeled out his party's specific demands to Modi government: "By now, Pakistan should already have been declared a terrorist state. We demand, there should be a special session of Parliament to discuss our security situation and to declare Pakistan a terrorist state."Singhvi further demanded: "We demand the considerable scaling down of Pak high commission in Delhi and our high commission in Pakistan."
