Known for his dedication to his party organisation, 57-year-old Trivendra Singh Rawat has seen many ups and downs in his two-decade-old political career. His best quality is that he has been very patient and has survived despite facing very adverse political situations. This singular quality may have played a crucial role in his elevation as the new CM of Uttarakhand. 

Trivendra never misses an opportunity to make his presence felt in the BJP’s state office during his stay in Dehradun. He interacts with party workers regularly and gives them a hearing. While other leaders in the CM race stayed in Delhi after the massive win, Rawat preferred to spend his time at the BJP’s state headquarters. 

In the last five years, Trivendra faced many challenges. He lost the assembly polls in Raipur in 2012. Two years later, he could not even win the byelection from Doiwala, vacated by former CM Ramesh Pokhriyal after his election to Lok Sabha . His bad electoral run, however, ended when he was elected from Doiwala this time. 

A native of Khairasain village in Pauri Garhwal district, Rawat associated himself with the RSS in 1979. In 1985, he became an RSS pracharak and worked in Dehradun. In 1993, he was elevated as BJP Sangathan Mantri. 

After Uttarakhand became a separate state in 2000, he remained BJP’s organisational secretary till 2002. He was elected to the assembly in 2002 and 2007. After losing polls in 2012, he was made BJP’s national secretary a year later. 

Soon, he was dispatched to Jharkhand for strengthening the party organisation. Rawat played a role in BJP’s victory in the state. The challenges in store as the CM of a debt-ridden state are many. 

The first will be to generate revenue and usher in development before the 2019 Lok Sabha polls. Uttarakhand’s excise department is one of the major sources of income. Outgoing chief minister Harish Rawat tried to set up distilleries at Devprayag and Bhimtal. “People, particularly women , opposed Rawat’s attempts to set up distilleries. 

So, it would not be easy task for the new CM to go on the lines of the Harish Rawat government,” a senior official said. There is also a debate on the mining policy as environmentalists have stressed need for ecological balance. The state bureaucracy is another problem as several officers control different departments. 

When the Char Dham Yatra begins next month, Rawat will need to restore the confidence of tourist to travel to the hills. His task begins now because most voters may not be as patient as him.
