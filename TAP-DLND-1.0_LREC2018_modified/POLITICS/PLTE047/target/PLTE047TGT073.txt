
Claiming that the Jat protesters were changing their goalposts, Chief Minister Manohar Lal Khattar today said his government was ready to constitute a new committee of the Cabinet or the House to settle their “legitimate” demands. Dissatisfied with Khattar’s reply on the adjournment motion of the Indian National Lok Dal (INLD), Congress and Independent MLA Jai Prakash on the indefinite dharnas of Jats, the INLD staged a walkout. Acrimonious scenes between Treasury benches and the Opposition were witnessed during the discussion, which lasted over two hours. Seeking the cooperation of opposition parties in resolving the issue, Khattar said the protesters had submitted a 28-point demand charter to the officers’ committee which was holding talks with them. “They began with 11 demands, only to add 17 more. They demanded that an officers’ committee be constituted for talks and they have announced that they don’t want to talk to the committee anymore. We are trying our best to reach out to the protesters. I am willing to be a part of a committee if that satisfies them,” Khattar said in his reply to the motion. Appreciative of the protesters’ peaceful dharnas across the state, Khattar did not miss the opportunity to take a swipe at the “non-violent” protesters. “I am grateful that they are protesting peacefully, but there are two sides to non-violence. While Mahatma Gandhi used it to lead the country to freedom, it can also be used as a smokescreen on platforms used to make objectionable remarks,” he said. “We get reports of such statements and this cannot be termed as non-violent,” he said, urging protesters to come forth for talks and not vitiate the situation with provocative comments. On the main demands of protesters, Khattar said the government had constituted a committee to discuss police cases against innocents and warned of action in case peace was breached. “On job offers to those who lost their lives during last year’s agitation, 25 applications were received, of which 18 refused the offer letters,” he said. “We are willing to appoint them at the DC rate and will adjust them. Regarding cases of violence during the agitation, proceedings to release the innocent are underway and the guilty have been arrested,” Khattar stated. He admitted that last year’s violence had created a wedge in society and conceded that there was distrust between communities. In response to Leader of Opposition Abhay Chautala’s persistent demand on action against BJP MP Raj Kumar Saini, the Chief Minister said that they had done whatever was possible. “He is an MP and any further action can be initiated only by the Lok Sabha,” he clarified. He challenged Chautala to get an FIR registered against the MP if he was dissatisfied while urging the leaders to exercise restraint in making personal comments. Earlier, Congress MLA and former Speaker Raghvir Singh Kadian said the government’s indifference had forced members of the Jat community to sit on dharna after the government failed to fulfil its commitment made to them last year. He suggested that the government should constitute a committee of the House to talk to the protesters and accept their demands. Independent MLA Jai Prakash said Jats felt “betrayed” by the BJP government, which had gone back on its promise of fulfilling their demands. He suggested that the government write a letter to the Union government on dropping of all CBI cases. Abhay, while giving specific instances where no arrests had been made with regard to last year’s violence, suggested that the government talk to the protesters directly, accept their demands and settle the matter.
