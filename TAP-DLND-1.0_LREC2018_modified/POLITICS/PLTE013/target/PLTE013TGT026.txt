Over three crore people formed a human chain formed Bihar to show their support on prohibition of liquor ban in the state.
This was beyond expectations even for the chief minister Nitish Kumar, who asked the Guinness book of world records to take notice of the mammoth gathering.
'As per reports coming from all across the state, more than three crore citizens have participated in the human chain programme today,' Nitish told reporters hours after the event.

The 'unexpected' rush of participants resulted in increase in distance on determined route of human chain to 11,400 km, he said.
According to NDTV , Bihar Chief Secretary Anjani Kumar Singh claimed that they were forming 'the world's longest human chain of over 11,000 km' to support prohibition of liquor in the state.
Three satellites, including one foreign and two of Indian Space Research Organisation, four aircraft, two helicopters and 40 drones obtained images of the human chain, officials said.
Initially, it was estimated that more than two crore citizens would participate in the human chain spread across 11,292 km.

The CM started the human chain by joining hands with RJD President Lalu Prasad on the one side and state assembly speaker Vijay Chaudhary on the other side, for 45 minutes.
The chain branched out from Gandhi Maidan into different directions and spread all across the state.
'At present, the Bihar population is between 11 to 12 crore, and out of this more than 3 crore coming on the streets to form human chain, which clearly shows that people of Bihar have taken a vow against liquor,' the CM said.
Calling the campaign a massive hit, Kumar said directions of the Patna High Court was taken care of and no hindrance was caused in running of essential services.
Asked if the state government would stake claim to Guinness book of world record for registration of largest human chain in world so far, Kumar said: 'Why shall we make any claim...if Guinness book has to remain Guinness book it itself will take note of this congregation of people in form of human chain.'
According to official records, the human chain in Bihar has surpassed the 1050km-long chain formed in Bangladesh in 2004.

Political leaders, cutting across party lines, participated in the event.
Legislative council chairman Awdesh Narayan Singh, deputy chief minister Tejaswi Yadav, state Congress President and minister Ashok Choudhary, NCP general secretary and MP Tariq Anwar and a host of ministers and legislators held each other's hand.
Opposition BJP which lent support to the human chain joined the queue in Siwan.
To display communal harmony, four children dressed in traditional attires of Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs and Christians stood by sides of the CM and Lalu Prasad.
RJD supremo Lalu Prasad told reporters all parties and citizens in general were together to fight the menace of liquor.
Congress state president Ashok Choudhary, whose education department was the organiser, said it was the biggest social congregation of people after Independence.
