Protesting against the crackdown on illegal and mechanised slaughter houses in Uttar Pradesh, meat sellers have gone on an indefinite strike in Lucknow. As residents and hotels struggled to meet their meat needs, fish emerged as the only recourse, leading to an unexpected hike in demand. Crowds surge at fish markets As meat shops remained closed across Lucknow, fish markets received an unprecedented crowd of buyers. The regular buyers, Bengalis, were outnumbered at Kaiserbagh fish mandi and stock that lasts entire day ended by 3pm. Despite 30-50% increase in prices, people bought all kinds of fishes. Vendors were also seen teaching first timers how to cook fish. Fish the only recourse to salvage menus People from adjoining districts flocked the fish markets in the city too. Most customers were those who had wedding or religious ceremonies scheduled next week. "The menu was planned in advance but things went awry after the meat sellers' strike. Though mutton and chicken is available in some districts, we are not getting it the quantity required, hence we decided to put fish on the menu," said Mohammad Faheem from Barabanki. Fish gets dearer As the demand for fish went up by leaps and bounds following the strike, the prices have also surged. Hilsa, the beloved fish of Bengalis which was priced at Rs 1,000, has gone up to Rs 1,400 per kg. Prawns, which came at Rs 600 per kg only last Sunday, are now charged at Rs 1,200. Rohu, which cost Rs 250 till the previous week, is dearer by Rs 130, whereas Pomfret witnessed a 25 per cent rise in rates and is now sold at Rs 800 per kg. Better prepared than hungry Some people began stocking up on non-vegetarian staples as soon as they got to know about the strike by the butchers' association. "I have stuffed my freezer with meat. On the very first day of the strike, I had bought two dozen packs of processed meat from the departmental store nearby," said Arjit Sinha, a businessman. College-goer Saurabh Shukla said, "I have requested one of my friends in the Army to bring me packed frozen meat from their canteen and I hope there is ample stock. It is so difficult to survive only on vegetables." Strike spoils feast before the fast The weekend coincided with eve of Navaratra and the meat vendors' strike disappointed lakhs of non-vegetarians in the city, who had planned on bingeing on meat delicacies before the nine-day abstinence. Most restaurants have stopped serving non-veg following scarcity of meat, while outlets of multi-national fast-food chains have seen a huge rush since the meat crisis in state. Missing meat takes spice off weddings The strike by the butchers' association has led to an acute shortage of meat. Since the strike coincides with the wedding season, many Muslim families will be forced to tone down their celebration. While most of the caterers have given in to the unavailability of buffalo meat and shortage of mutton and chicken and are refusing meat items, the top ones have just hiked their rates. Navaratra to save blushes but till when? Most small restaurant and hotels in Lucknow have stopped serving non-vegetarian dishes. Some have increased vegetarian items on the menu while others are coping with their reserved stock of meat. Their only consolation is that the upcoming Navaratra festival is a time when sale of nonvegetarian dishes goes down any way. Meanwhile, bigger hotels in the city are coping with reserved stock of meat. "But if the strike is not called off beyond Navratri, even five-star hotels would be in trouble," the head chef at one such place said. Big blow for Lucknow institutions It has all gone quiet in Lucknow's iconic Tunday Kababi which was once flooded with patrons seeking out their melt-in-the-mouth Galawati Kababs. The ban on illegal slaughterhouses by the new BJP government in Uttar Pradesh has left the owners with no choice but to make a drastic change in their signature dish for the first time in 120 years -- replacing buffalo meat with the costlier chicken and mutton. The result? A nearly 80 per cent drop in footfall. It is the same story for another over-century old eatery, Rahim's, which too had to switch to chicken and mutton for their trademark 'Nihari' and 'Pasanda', dishes usually made of buffalo meat. Big cats restless as meat supply dwindles Zoo authorities have been forced to switch over to mutton and chicken which is not going down well with the big cats and is also burdening the exchequer. Zoo authorities said they require 235 kgs of buffalo meat every day, but the supply for the past two days is just 80 kgs. Officials added that mutton and chicken was also not an adequate diet for the big cats due to low fat content. Deprived of their regular diet, the animals are becoming restless and even refusing to eat the chicken and mutton being served at meal times. A first for non-veg fame Lucknow Old-timers say they've never witnessed such an acute crisis of meat in Lucknow, which is known for its non-vegetarian cuisine. "On Sunday morning, I went out to buy mutton but couldn't find a single shop open and I couldn't believe it," retired government official Nasir said. People as old as 93 couldn't think of one occasion when meat was not available in the market. "This is certainly the first time the city is facing such a crisis that neither raw nor cooked meat is available and I think the government has taken too strong a step without providing any alternative," said Girdhari Lal, a retired teacher.
