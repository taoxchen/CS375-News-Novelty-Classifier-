 The city of Nawabs, known for its mouth-watering Awadhi delicacies, has been forced to turn vegetarian as over 5,000 shops selling mutton and chicken in the city are on a strike in protest against the BJP government's crackdown against unlicensed outlets. Showing solidarity, famous non-vegetarian joints of the city like Tunday's and Mubeen's, too, downed their shutters on Friday. 

The strike starting from Lucknow is now threatening to spread across the state. "We have appealed to our counterparts in other parts of the state to the strike," said Iqbal Qureshi, president of Meat Murga Vyapar Kalyan Samiti (MMVKS), UP. Murga Vypar Mandal of Kanpur has already announced to join the strike from Monday. The supply of buffalo meat has already almost stopped from Tuesday itself. 

The decision to stop meat supply was taken at a meeting of Lucknow Murga Mandi Samiti (LMMS) and MBVKS in the state capital. LMMS represents more than 50 wholesalers who supply chicken to about 5,000 retailers, besides restaurants, hotels and small food joints. 

MBVKS represents about 600 dealers who supply goats and chicken to retailers, shops, restaurants and hotels in the entire city. LMMS representative Sanjay Saxena said applications for licences of many dealers were gathering dust at Lucknow Municipal Corporation (LMC) since 2010. 

"We were forced to work without licence because LMC is neither issuing fresh licences, not renewing old ones for three years. Our legal business turned illegal because of apathy of the authorities and now the crackdown has hit our livelihood," said Saxena. 

Refuting the allegations, Lucknow Municipal Corporation's veterinary officer AK Rao said, "Of all 602 licences, 340 are renewed and approximately 130 have been cancelled because they were flouting the Supreme Court guidelines, according to which no butcher shop should be adjacent to religious places and educational institutions. The rest of the licences were for shops that sell buffalo meat. We cannot issue a licence to them because there is no legitimate slaughterhouse in the city. So, as of now, there is no pendency." 

"There should be a designated market for meat sellers outside city limits. The government must give us place where it is legally correct. We are ready to hire or buy the premises so that our business is not affected," added Qureshi. 

Other demands of the association include setting up of a modern 'murga mandi', a centralised market for broiler chicken. 

Another member said the BJP government should have created an alternative mechanism before the crackdown. Action should also be taken against LMC officials, he added. 

Agitated members of the two associations also said most of the roadside dhabas, fast food vendors, paan shops and vegetable/fruit vendors and retailers in the city are in business without licence but no action has been taken against them. They said it was the fault of officials and not those in business for livelihood. 

In Bareilly, while 60-odd licensed mutton shops were open on Friday, a large number of unauthorised outlets which had mushroomed on the roadside remained closed out of the fear of a crackdown. In Agra, both mutton and chicken were available but supply was very low.
