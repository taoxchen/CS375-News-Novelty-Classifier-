Ravindra Gaikwad, the Shiv Sena MP who slapped an Air India official after an argument over his seating arrangement was forced to take a train back to Pune from Delhi, after the Air India cancelled his ticket and private carriers black listed him from flying. 

The MP who was bragging about "going back to Pune on the same flight" was forced to look for other options on Friday as it became evident that none of the airlines were willing to accommodate him . 

While onboard the train, the MP who was till hours ago boasting about how he slapped a 60-year-old man seemed mellowed down. Refraining to comment Gaikwad said party chief Uddhav Thackeray or another Shiv Sena leader Anil Desai will comment on it. 
The MP however deboarded the train in Mathura after "feeling unwell". 
Both the MP and his party still remains unrepentant and is demanding an apology from the AI staffer for the incident. 
"What would happen if the public decides to blacklist the airline," Sena spokesperson Sanjay Raut asked. 
Meanwhile the government said it is examining the 'legality' of the blacklisting of the MP by Air India, IndiGo, Jet Airways, SpiceJet, Go Air , Vistara and AirAsia India. 
Union minister of state for aviation Jayant Sinha said the government is looking into whether it was done within the legal framework. 
"The airlines have advised us that they would not be willing to carry this particular passenger (Gaikwad). We are studying their inputs on this matter. This is an action they have taken independently and we are examining it. Any action undertaken has to be lawful; it has to fit in with the general provisions of the law and the Aircraft Act. We are seeing how this whole approach should work. We have to see if this action of the airlines is within the framework of law."
