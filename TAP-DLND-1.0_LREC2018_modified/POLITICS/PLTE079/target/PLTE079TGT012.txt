Apart from Rohith 's friends and family, the mammoth congregation at UoH campus on Tuesday comprised Dalit victims from Dadri and Una and JNU students. While they were initially stopped from entering campus at the main road leading to the university, cops later allowed them to join the rest of the agitators. Speaking on the occasion, a teary-eyed Radhika Vemula, Rohith's mother, said, "I just want to hug my son's stupa and pay him a tribute. I am not a terrorist or a goon who would hurt anyone. Why is the UoH administration afraid of allowing me inside? How can I harm them now, if I haven't done anything against these people when they killed my son?" Jaan Mohammad Saifi, brother of Mohammad Akhlaq, who was lynched by a mob in Dadri in 2015 for allegedly storing beef in his house, asked Dalits and Muslims to come together to fight the "Hindu" government. "What UoH students are doing now is not 'hungama'. This is the need of the hour," Saifi said. While Rahul Sonpimple, a JNU student who was suspended just like Rohith, said: "If any Dalit student is unhappy on campus, we will make sure there is no peace in the university." Jeetu Sarvaiya, the only college-going dalit in from village Mota Samadhiyala who gave victims of Una attack the courage to fight back, said Radhika might have lost one son, but has gained lakhs in return. "Rohith is alive in our hearts and we will fight for him and other fellow Dalits till our last breath," Sarvaiya said. Despite brief turmoil on campus, students went ahead with their 'Shahadath Din' programme (to mark Rohith's first death anniversary) as per schedule. Those close to the deceased scholar visited all branches inside the university and paid their tributes by garlanding in photographs. In a statement issued late in the evening, UoH administration maintained that all academic activities, including teaching, went on without any interruption during the day.
