
Classes at Delhi University's prestigious Ramjas College were today suspended following violent clashes between two rival student unions - Left-backed All India Students Association (AISA) and RSS-backed Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP). 
The campus had almost turned into a battleground yesterday with students resorting to violent clashes including hitting each other with hockey sticks that left many of them bleeding. 
The college authorities suspended classes for the entire day today and to maintain peace inside the campus police personnel were deployed outside DU arts faculty. 
Police said an FIR under various IPC sections of rioting and assault of public servant has been lodged against unknown persons based on the version of the SHO of the Maurice Nagar police station and not on the basis of complaints by AISA-backed students and teachers belonging to Delhi University Teachers Association. 
Investigation by two additional DCPs has also been ordered. Seven police personnel, including the SHO of Maurice Nagar, teachers and journalists had also sustained injuries. College principal Rajender Prasad said he is in discussion with the teachers, who were part of organisers of the event, and requested both the groups to not disrupt the peace and harmony in the college. The genesis of the clash was an invite to JNU students Umar Khalid, facing sedition charges, and Shehla Rashid to address a seminar on 'Culture of Protests' which was withdrawn by the college authorities two days ago following opposition by the ABVP. The clashes took place around 1 PM and police could bring the situation under control five hours later. A group of students were also locked up allegedly by ABVP activists for four hours at the college campus. The ABVP supporters had yesterday also resorted to violence following which the college authorities withdrew the invitation to Rashid and Khalid, triggering widespread anger among a section of students and teachers who had planned a march today to Maurice Nagar police station, demanding action against those involved in the "vandalism". On their part, the ABVP members did not allow the march to proceed and allegedly locked up the students and teachers inside the college campus.At this point, some AISA members tried to barge inside the college to "rescue the captives" resulting in the clashes. "Not only the ABVP completely disrupted the seminar, they were also abusing, beating women and even journalists leaving some profusely bleeding. Why can't the police see such hooliganism? There is adequate police presence on the campus but nobody from ABVP has been detained yet," alleged Rashid, who was formerly JNU Students' Union vice president. She also alleged that "police joined the ABVP goons" in attacking her.Abha Dev, a member of DU Teachers' Association, claimed that besides "several students", three teachers of different DU colleges -- Prasanta Chakraborty (English), Suvrita (History) and Mousumi Bose (Philosophy) -- were injured in the clashes and taken to Hindu Rao hospital.
