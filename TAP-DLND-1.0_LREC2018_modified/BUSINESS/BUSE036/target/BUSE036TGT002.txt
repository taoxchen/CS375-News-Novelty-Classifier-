Snapchat chief executive officer Evan Spigel lampooned claims made by an ex-employee on Saturday, saying that the executive had claimed at a meeting in 2015, that the company wasn't interested in expanding in 'poor countries like India'.
In a suit filed by ex-employee Anthony Pompliano, uploaded by by user gmaddaus on Scribd, he (Pompliano) alleged that he was abruptly cut short by the Spigel at a meeting in 2015, when he had brought up overseas growth of the app in developing countries. 
Snapchat - the video based social media platform - owned by parent company Snap Inc., responded to the report saying, "This is ridiculous. Obviously Snap is for everyone! It's available worldwide to download for free." 
Pompliano was allegedly fired from Snapchat and is a former Facebook employee. 
According to the report, "This app is only for rich people. I don't want to expand in poor countries like India and Spain," Spigel had said. 
Pompliano's claims were made in a lawsuit, a copy of which was uploaded by Scribd user gmaddaus. In the suit, which was filed after the complainant was fired only three weeks after he worked at the social media company, among other allegations, he claimed he was "lured away from Facebook, to run Snapchat's new user growth and engagement team by falsely representing to him, among other things, the company's growth." According to him, in the copy of the law suit, he was presented with metrics throughout the recruiting process, (redacted text) both of which were false."
To this, Snap said, "Snap did not give investors misstated user metrics back in 2015; nor did Snap employees commit any of the panoply of alleged bad acts that litter Pompliano’s complaint. Snap will demonstrate as much at the appropriate time in the appropriate forum." A copy of the suit filed by Snap was also uploaded on Scribd by the same user. 
While Pompliano has alleged that he was fired and his chances of getting another job were maligned by the company, Snap said, not only did Pompliano get a job after he was fired from Snap, he was soon fired from there for poor job performance. "He (had) filed a complaint reciting vague allegation of fraud against the Brighten Labs Inc too."
Clearing its stand, the company said, "Snap has nothing to hide, especially now that it is a public company."
