UK set to follow US in banning electronic devices from flights The ban is set to affect airlines flying into the UK from a handful of airports in the Middle East and North Africa.
The UK is poised to follow the US in announcing a ban on electronics devices from being carried on some aircraft flying into the country. 
The Department of the Transport told CNET on Tuesday afternoon it expected to make an announcement later in the day regarding the ban. The exact details may differ from those in the ban issued by the Department of Homeland Securit y in the US late Monday. 
Devices bigger than a phone, including tablets and laptops are restricted under the US ban, which applies only to aircraft flying into the country. They will not be allowed in aircraft cabins, and must instead be checked into the hold. Flights from ten airports in eight Muslim countries across North Africa and the Middle East are affected, with Gulf carriers Qatar, Emirates and Etihad set to be hit especially hard. 
US officials told journalists the ban wasn't in response to a specific threat, but rather "evaluated intelligence" pointing to terrorists "aggressively pursuing" innovative ways to carry out attacks, reported ZDNet . 
UK security officials have seen the same intelligence as their counterparts at US agencies and have been formulating plans for the ban over the last few weeks, according to sources speaking to the Telegraph .
