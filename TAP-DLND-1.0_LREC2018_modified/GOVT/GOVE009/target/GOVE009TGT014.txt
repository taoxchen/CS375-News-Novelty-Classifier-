 India is looking at the possible impact of Australia's decision to abolish a work visa scheme popular with Indian nationals and the issue may affect Indo-Australian free trade talks. Indian citizens are the largest group of workers hired in Australia under the 457 visa programme. "We will no longer allow 457 visa to be passports to jobs that could and should go to Australians," Australian Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull said. He said the move is intended to tackle unemployment and "Australian workers must have priority for Australian jobs." The 457 visa allows businesses to bring temporary foreign workers into Australia for up to four years. The changes would not affect current visa holders numbering more than 95,000, according to Australian media. Indian nationals make up about 25 per cent of these workers and New Delhi wants Australia to accept more professionals from India. The Indian government is "examining consequences" of the new Australian policy in consultation with all stakeholders, Ministry of External Affairs spokesman Gopal Baglay said on Tuesday. "This is also a matter we will be looking at in the context of Comprehensive Economic Cooperation Agreement negotiations (with Australia)," he said. The Australian move comes amid a crackdown by United States President Donald Trump's administration on H-1B work visas, which are heavily used by technology companies to hire foreign workers. Indian professionals are among the biggest beneficiaries of the US visa scheme as well.
