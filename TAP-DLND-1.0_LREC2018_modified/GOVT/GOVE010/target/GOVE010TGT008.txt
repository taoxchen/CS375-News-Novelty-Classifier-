
The Maharashtra Government is considering de-notifying state highways that run through various cities in the state in order to beat the Supreme Court's ban on liquor shops, bars and restaurants alongside these facilities.
Maharashtra Excise Minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule said on Monday that
the state government was considering several options, including handing over highways passing through cities to their respective local bodies.
The minister said the government was likely to suffer a loss of Rs 7000 crore a year after the apex court banned shops, restaurants and bars from serving liquor within 500 metres of highways.
The state government has already announced a waiver of transfer fees to establishments that are moving 500 metres away from the highways because of the ban. However, representatives of hotel owners and bars have told the government that their members have invested lakhs of rupees in their properties and it would not be easy
to move to new locations.
According to Indian Hotel and Restaurant Association (AHAR), more than 500 restaurants and permit rooms have been forced to shut in Mumbai alone. Two highways, the Western and Eastern Express Highways, run through the city and hundreds of bars have sprung up on either side of these arterial roads. These highways are maintained by the state Public Works Department and the Maharashtra Government is considering
denotifying them and handing them over to either the Mumbai Municipal Corporation and the Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority, sources said.
Adarsh Shetty, president, AHAR, said though the laws allow these establishments to continue serving food without alcohol, most of them have shut down for the now.
