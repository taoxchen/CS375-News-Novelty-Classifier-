 The Maharashtra government, facing an estimated loss of Rs 7,000 crore per year following the Supreme Court’s verdict banning liquor shops within 500 metres of highways, is mulling denotifying highways in the State. 
Thousands of watering holes, iconic clubs and pubs across the country have closed shop after the apex court’s order. Maharashtra Excise Minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule had earlier said in the State Assembly that alternative ways to increase the revenue would be explored. 
The minister had made it clear that if highways passing through cities and bypasses were handed over to the respective local bodies, it would help keep the bars and liquor shops along these roads open. He had also announced waiver of transfer fees for those affected establishments that were willing to shift their premises beyond the 500 metres limit from the highways. 
The states of Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and Chandigarh have denotified the State highways, following which they will not be expected to close down liquor outlets, permit rooms and bars along such roads as specified by the Supreme Court.
