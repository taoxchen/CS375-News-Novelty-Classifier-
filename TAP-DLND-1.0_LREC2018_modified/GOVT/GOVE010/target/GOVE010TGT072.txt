Dealing a major blow to hotel and restaurants serving liquor along highways, the Supreme Court (SC) on Friday said liquor vendors within a distance of 500 metres from highways will not be allowed to operate.
The Supreme Court was hearing a bunch of petitions from some states, bars and hotel associations seeking a modification to its December 2016 order banning liquor vendors within 500 metres of state and national highways across the country from April 1.
The associations said the new court order would lead to a major loss of revenue for the State government.
With the fresh order in place, hotels and restaurants having licences for permit rooms will not be allowed to sell liquor.
 In case of smaller towns, where the population is less than 20,000, the SC has made a concession of 220 metres instead of 500 metres.
Pune Hotel Restaurant Owner’s Association President Ganesh Shetty said there are 1,950 permit rooms in the district. Commenting on the order, Shetty said there will be a major revenue loss to the government.
He said, “Many stretches of state or national highways are being maintained by municipal corporations for years together. How can these stretches of roads be called state or national highways?” Shetty added that only 10 per cent of permit rooms are actually located along highways.
Many states such as Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, and Punjab had sought a relaxation of this limit. Attorney General Mukul Rohatgi had stated in December 2016 that the order did not apply to hotel and restaurant owners but the Supreme Court rejected his view.
In its earlier order issued in December 2016, the SC had called for a ban on all liquor shops along all highways and made it clear that licences of the existing shops will not be renewed after March 31.
State Excise Department (Pune District) Superintendent Mohan Varde told Sakal Times that the department was seeking clarity on the order passed on Friday. There is a provision in the Supreme Court order on deferment of implementation of the order by six months. But it is not clear whether the clause is applicable to Maharashtra. Varde said, “Our higher authorities will inform us whether this clause is applicable to the State. We will implement the orders issued by the higher courts, verify and prepare the list of establishments selling liquor within 500 metres from highways and the implementation of apex court’s order will be done accordingly.”
Varde added that with this order in place, hotels and restaurants, which have permit room licence will also not be allowed to sell liquor.
As per the earlier order, the Excise Department had conducted a survey, which revealed that around 675 liquor shops existed in Pune district, which included beer, wine, and country liquor shops.
PERMIT ROOM OWNERS PAY LICENCE FEES
Surprisingly, many permit room owners have already paid their fees in order to renew their licences for the financial year following a letter from a senior government officer on March 31. The letter released on Friday assured that there would not be any change for licences for Foreign Liquor III and IV category, referring to Attorney General Mukul Rohatgi’s opinion during the earlier hearing.
