The Supreme Court on Friday modified its order banning liquor vends within 500 metres on national and state highways, reducing the distance to 220 metres in areas having a population of up to 20,000. The reduced distance criterion is also applicable for liquor vends along highways in hilly states like Sikkim, Meghalaya and Himachal Pradesh. A bench headed by Chief Justice J SKhehar, however, made it clear that its December 15, 2016 order banning such vends within 500 metres of highways shall remain operative for areas other than those specified in the new order. The bench clarified that licences given to liquor vendors prior to the December 15 judgement would be valid up to September 30 this year while the rest of the outlets would have to shut from today. The bench, also comprising Justices D Y Chandrachud and L N Rao, said that the verdict was given keeping in mind road accidents owing to drunken driving. The apex court’s decision came on pleas seeking a relook at its verdict banning liquor vends within 500 metres of national and state highways from April 1, with Attorney General Mukul Rohatgi having said that the judgement needs rectification as the “budget of the states will go for a six”.
