 Following the Centre's blow to VIP culture by banning the use of red beacons on cars by May 1, Maharashtra's ministers were soon trying to outdo each other in removing the contraptions from their vehicles on Wednesday itself. 

Initially, chief minister Devendra Fadnavis said he would stop using the lal batti by Thursday but with several cabinet ministers removing it on Wednesday itself, he quickly changed his mind and did the same. "To welcome such an epic step for strengthening our democracy, I resolve to stop using the red beacon atop my car," he tweeted. 

Cabinet ministers who removed the lal batti on Wednesday included finance minister Sudhir Mungantiwar, education minister Vinod Tawde, energy minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule and transport minister Diwakar Raote. Bawankule's office sent out a message saying he had removed the red beacon while on tour to Ralegan Siddhi. 

Transport minister Raote, who belongs to the Shiv Sena, said, "You should show your status and importance through your work, not symbols like lal batti." 

Politicians across party lines publicly welcomed the move, though they privately agreed that the loss of this coveted status symbol would be a disappointment for those in power. 

"Most ministers will be disappointed, though they will not say it," said former minister and NCP leader Jayant Patil. "I feel it's a great move. The lal batti gave importance to ministers and the public was getting angry at this special treatment. The red beacon was also being misused as a status symbol," he said. 

Congress leaders pointed out that Punjab chief minister Amarinder Singh who belongs to their party had already implemented the move in his state. "I had stopped using the red beacon when I was chief minister because I realized it's not required. Dignitaries, including the President, the Prime Minister and chief ministers have escort cars. They do not need the red beacon," said former chief minister Prithviraj Chavan from Congress. "The read beacon is a relic of the British Raj and this step was long overdue," he said. 

Senior bureaucrat Praveen Pardeshi said that he, too, welcomed the move. "I never switch on the beacon light. It draws too much attention and the public resents it."
