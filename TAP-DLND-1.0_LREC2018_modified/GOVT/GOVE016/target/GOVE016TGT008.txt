In an unexpected development, Union Finance Minister and Defence Minister Arun Jaitley announced today that VVIP vehicles will no longer be allowed to have red beacons on them.
The use of red, white and blue beacons on vehicles in India is enshrined in Rule 108 of the Motor Vehicles Act. According to sub rules I and III, the central and state governments can specify which categories of dignitaries are eligible for installing red beacons on their vehicles, Jaitley explained. "The Centre is abolishing this rule from the rule book," he said. "This will mean that there will be no dignitary whom either the state or central government can allow to use red beacons."
The rule goes into effect from May 1 , he said. "The rule that gives the central and state govt the power to specify these dignitaries is being abolished."
Jaitley then went on to discuss sub rule II of Rule 108, which gives state governments the right to specify which vehicle can carry the blue beacon. Jaitley said now the blue beacons will be allowed only for defined emergency services.
"This rule is being changed and once it is changed, only defined emergency services - such as ambulance, fire brigade or police vehicle - will have the provision to install blue beacons," said Jaitley.
Jaitley also said that the emergency services will be defined in the new rule and there won't be any exceptions made for either red or blue beacons.
