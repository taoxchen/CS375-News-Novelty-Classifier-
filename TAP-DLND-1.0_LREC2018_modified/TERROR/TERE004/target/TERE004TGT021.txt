Five people died and eight others were wounded following a shooting by a reported lone gunman at the Fort Lauderdale/Hollywood Airport on Friday afternoon, according to law enforcement authorities. 
The suspected gunman is in custody, according to the Broward Sheriff's Office , which late Friday reported five dead, eight “initially” injured, and 37 others injured after the shooting. 
CNN, citing law enforcement sources, reported that the suspected gunman had a gun checked in his baggage and had declared the firearm to authorities — both of which are allowed under aviation security rules. They said the suspect flew to Florida from Alaska, and upon arrival at Fort Lauderdale retrieved his bag at baggage claim, went to the bathroom to get the gun out and opened fire. 
Not only are air passengers allowed to carry guns and ammunition in their checked luggage, but authorities said the shooting occurred in an unsecured part of the airport — outside the checkpoints screened by the Transportation Security Administration. That means that theoretically, it wouldn’t be difficult for anyone to walk into any airport with a concealed gun and carry out the same kind of attack. 
Airports’ unsecured areas have been the locations of several major attacks in recent years, including two deadly suicide bombings last March in Brussels and a November 2013 incident in which a gunman killed a TSA officer inside Los Angeles International. Since the LAX shooting, union leaders and some lawmakers have called for the placing of armed officers at airport checkpoints , but that has never come to fruition. 
Sen. Bill Nelson said the gunman in Friday’s attack was carrying a military ID with the name Esteban Santiago, though it's not clear whether it belonged to him or to someone else, according to the Associated Press . He said investigators have not determined a motive for the shooting. The gunman is reportedly from Naples, Florida. 
CNN reported that Santiago joined the National Guard in Puerto Rico in 2007 and had served in Iraq for 10 months, beginning in April 2010. A spokeswoman for the Alaska Army National Guard told CNN that he had been in the Army Reserves before joining the guard there in Alaska in November 2014 and was discharged in August for unsatisfactory performance. 
CNN also reported that Santiago appeared several times at the FBI office in Anchorage and that he had told agents that he had been hearing “voices in his head, including some telling him to join ISIS.” 
“Sounds like he’s a mental case, not a terror-inspired case,” Nelson told CNN’s Wolf Blitzer. 
CBS News, also citing law enforcement sources, identified the gunman as Esteban Santiago-Ruiz, 26, and that he was carrying active-duty military identification, and was born in New Jersey. 
Because people can legally and easily enter airports with guns in jurisdictions with open-carry and concealed-carry laws, the gunman could have executed his rampage without even exercising his right to check the weapon aboard an airplane. But his methods illustrate the struggle facing TSA and law-enforcement officials trying to keep airports and the flying public safe, especially when thousands of people come to airports each year with loaded firearms. 
In 2015, TSA caught a record 2,653 guns — 83 percent of which were loaded — that travelers tried to bring through security checkpoints within carry-on baggage. The protocol is for TSA officers to inform local law enforcement upon discovering a gun at an airport checkpoint. And in states where firearms are allowed, those travelers get off without punishment. 
Broward Sheriff Scott Israel told reporters at an afternoon news conference at the airport that the gunman in Friday's attack was apprehended by a BSO deputy without incident and uninjured. He said the suspect was being interrogated by FBI agents and BSO homicide investigators. 
It is not known whether the shooting was terror-related, said Israel, who added that there was no evidence of a second shooter, dismissing earlier reports of additional gun shots fired following the initial shooting. 
He said sheriff's deputies got a call around 12:55 p.m. about shots fired at the airport in the baggage claim area. Airport officials tweeted about "an ongoing incident in Terminal 2, Baggage Claim" and closed the airport. 
The Associated Press said that Miami area television stations showed video of medics taking care of a bleeding victim outside the airport. News helicopters hovering over the scene showed hundreds of people standing on the tarmac as an ambulance drove by and numerous law enforcement officers, including tactical units, rushed to the scene, the AP said. 
Gov. Rick Scott, who traveled from Fort Myers to Fort Lauderdale's Executive Airport, after hearing news of the shooting, said the "citizens of Florida will not tolerate senseless acts of evil.” 
"This was an attack on innocent people who were just trying to travel home to their families or go on a fun trip this weekend," Scott told reporters at a news conference broadcast live on major cable television networks. 
Scott said he reached out to President-elect Donald Trump and Vice-President-elect Mike Pence to discuss the shooting, but said he not spoken or reached out to President Barack Obama. 
When asked whether the shooting is evidence of the need to regulate firearms, Scott said “it’s not the time to do politics.” 
President-elect Donald Trump urged people to "stay safe" via Twitter. "Monitoring the terrible situation in Florida," he wrote on Twitter. "Just spoke to Gov. [Rick] Scott. Thoughts and prayers for all. Stay safe!" 
The shooting happened about 50 miles from Trump's part-time home in Palm Beach. It was apparently the worst mass shooting inside a U.S. airport in more than three years. 
White House homeland security adviser Lisa Monaco informed President Barack Obama about the shooting, National Security Council spokesman Ned Price said. 
Earlier in the day, Ari Fleischer, a press secretary for former U.S. President George W. Bush, described the shooting in a series of tweet via Twitter, tweeting that shots were fired and "everyone is running." 
"All seems calm now but the police aren't letting anyone out of the airport — at least not the area where I am," Fleischer posted on Twitter. 
South Florida congressional members reacted with shock and horror at the shooting, recalling fears of last summer's mass shooting at the Pulse nightclub in Orlando, which left 49 dead. The gunman, Omar Mateen, had pledged allegiance to the Islamic State. 
Law enforcement personnel stand outside a garage area at Fort Lauderdale–Hollywood International Airport Friday. 
Congressman Alcee Hastings, a Democrat from South Florida called the shooting a "cowardly act of violence." 
"My thoughts and prayers go out to the victims, and I offer my full support to the authorities on scene investigating this shooting," he said in a statement. 
Miami Congressman Mario Diaz-Balart expressed similar sentiments, saying he will "work with local, state, and federal authorities as they conduct their investigations." He chairs the Transportation, Housing and Urban Development Appropriations Subcommittee. 
U.S. Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz, whose district includes the airport, said she has been in contact with federal and local law enforcement authorities, but that "until we know more, it’s not prudent to speculate on what motivated this horrifying incident." 
“My thoughts are with the victims as well as the wounded and their families right now, and the countless others who were traumatized by this senseless violence," she said in a statement. "Sadly, I fear we have witnessed yet another disturbing moment in our nation’s ceaseless struggle with gun violence.”
