A senior al-Qaeda commander linked to major attacks in Pakistan, including the bombing of a luxury hotel and an assault on a cricket team, has been killed in a drone strike in Afghanistan, the Pentagon said. 
Qari Yasin, a member of the Tehrik- e-Taleban Pakistan group, was killed on March 19 in eastern Paktika province, the Pentagon added. 
"The death of Qari Yasin is evidence that terrorists who defame Islam and deliberately target innocent people will not escape justice," US Defense Secretary Jim Mattis said. 
Yasin, who went by several aliases, including Ustad Aslam, was accused of plotting the September 20, 2008 bombing on the Marriott Hotel in Islamabad that killed dozens of people, including two US service members. 
He was also said to have been behind a 2009 attack on a bus carrying the Sri Lankan cricket team in Lahore that killed six Pakistani police officers and two civilians, and wounded six members of the team. 
According to Pakistani "Most Wanted" lists he was also behind failed attempts to kill former president Pervez Musharraf in 2003 and former prime minister Shaukat Aziz in 2004. 
Security analyst Amir Rana said Yasin was the latest in a series of Pakistani militant fugitives to have been killed across the border in Afghanistan.
