
On the morning of June 18, long-time Acton resident Sri Krishna joined nearly 75 people to celebrate International Yoga Day at NARA Park in Acton. Krisha, who moved to the area for graduate school, spends his days working in robotics and raising his two children. 
“Being [in Massachusetts] has been incredible for my career and yoga is important to me because it helps me to relearn what I had in India. I am living the best of both cultures, said Krishna. 
The two-hour celebration was run by The Art of Living Foundation volunteers and used yoga, powerful breathing, and meditation to help attendees develop powerful relationships between their mind, body, soul, and the environment. 
The Art of Living Foundation is a nonprofit organization based in more than 150 countries. Events are being held around the globe to celebrate the third annual International Yoga Day. The actual Yoga Day is June 21, the first day of summer. 
The sun never sets on yoga, said Lina Shastri, Acton parent and event coordinator. Yoga is so important to relieve stress, especially for youth in Acton. 
A special yoga group was held for younger participants that included stretching and breathing exercises. 
Yoga is good for young children. It focuses their minds and the stretching relaxes their body, said Kavita, her eight-year-old daughter participated in children’s class. 
The event brought together people from every walk of life, some had been doing yoga for as long as they could remember and others had never done it before. 
Yoga helps you be self-aware and gives you time to slow down and relax and to find your inner strength and peace, said Krishna. Value yourself, then learn and grow all the time. There is no right way to do it, just do your best. 
After quietly meditating to the sounds of nature, the celebration finished with participants joining volunteers on the amphitheater stage for singing and dancing. 
Events like this are a great way to bring awareness to the community about the benefits of Yoga, said Shastri. We definitely want to do this again next year and possibly introduce it in the schools.
Erin Healy, a Littleton resident, attended the event with her friend and said she loved the event because it brought the community together in a place where everyone was happy.
