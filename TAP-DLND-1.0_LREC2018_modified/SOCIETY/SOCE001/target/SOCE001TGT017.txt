After government stepped in, Petrol Pump Association across India said they will accept credit or debit cards, differing to their earlier decision after banks announced 1 per cent fee on these transactions. If gas stations across India would have continued on their earlier statements then there were chances that people across India may have to face some huge problems due to non-availability of cash. According to Ajay Bansal, Chief of All-India Petroleum Dealers’ Association, said to NDTV that, “The move of not accepting credit or debit cards would have affected customers of all major banks including HDFC, Axis Bank, ICIC and many others. 
The protest of gas station against 1 per cent bank fee has been postponed till January 13, after both Union Government and respective State Government’s intervened in the matter, news agency ANI confirmed. Petroleum and Natural Gas Minister, Dharmendra Pradhan said, “Negotiation between bank and oil marketing companies are on. Surcharge will not be imposed on customers as well as on petrol pump owner. The issue will be resolved soon.” On the other side, ICICI bank spokesperson said, “We are not charging any transaction fee on card payment from tomorrow”. Both Union Government and State Government had intervened in the matter and there are chances that 1 per cent additional fee levied on gas stations on each transaction will be waived off. With the problem of cash across India, the decision taken by petrol-dealers could have hit the consumers as well as the government’s effort who is pushing its people for cashless transactions. On Sunday evening after meeting with gas station owners Ajay Bansal said, “We want to help the government in the digital and cashless drive. Our margins are already less. We want them to compensate us”. Bansal also claimed that he had received notices from HDFC, and Axis Bank about levying of MDR (Merchant Discount Rate). He said, “all banks which has come up with this 1 per cent scheme… won’t be able to operate their machines from midnight.” The All India Petroleum Dealers’ Association has also written to Union Finance Minister Arun Jaitley, saying, “HDFC and other banks had put the MDR charge in which the dealers will sustain a loss, as these charges will not be passed to consumers”. The letter also said about other bank disputes over online card transactions which also included delayed in exact settlements. According to the Delhi Petrol Dealers Association, “Earlier customer used to pay a surcharge to the bank, not the dealer. The banks are taking from customers now they want to take from us as well. What are they trying to do?”. The Delhi Petrol Dealers Association will also meet Finance Minister Arun Jaitley on Monday. Some reports suggest that the pumps have been notified that 1 per cent charge will be levied on all credit or debit Card Transactions between – 0.25 per cent and 1 per cent – from January 9, 2017. The banks quoted a circular issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) as the reason for extra charges. Since demonetisation, cashless transactions at gas stations have gone up and even the Union Government have been pushing people for digital transactions. Even there have been calls to stop the 2 per cent fees that the banks levy on credit card transaction.
