
Petrol station owners tonight deferred till January 13 their decision not to accept credit and debit card payments for fuel sales after banks put off the move to levy the transaction (MDR) charge. To promote cashless transactions, the government had waived the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on fuel purchase post-demonetisation for consumers. But after the expiry of the 50-day window, the banks decided to levy MDR on petrol station owners. Petrol station owners said they had been “informed by HDFC Bank that we will be charged 1 per cent on all credit card transactions and between 0.25 per cent and 1 per cent on all debit card transactions from January 9, 2017. The same will be debited to our account and net transaction value will be credited to our account”. This meant petrol pumps had to bear 1 per cent on all credit card transactions and between 0.25 per cent and 1 per cent on all debit card transactions from January 9. In protest, petrol pump operators decided not to accept card payments from Monday. “We have received official communication from oil marketing companies that the transaction fee charges have been deferred till January 13, 2017. AIPDA also has decided to defer the agitation till January 13,” said Ajay Bansal, president, All-India Petroleum Dealers Association (AIPDA).
