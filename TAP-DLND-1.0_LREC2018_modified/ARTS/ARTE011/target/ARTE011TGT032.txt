 "There was one performance this year that stunned me," she said. "It sank its hooks in my heart. Not because it was good. There was nothing good about it. But it was effective and it did its job. It made its intended audience laugh and show their teeth. It was that moment when the person asking to sit in the most respected seat in our country imitated a disabled reporter, someone he outranked in privilege, power and capacity to fight back."
In November 2015 while campaigning in South Carolina, Trump performed an unflattering impression of New York Times reporter Serge Kovaleski, who has a congenital condition that restricts movement of the muscles in his arms.
"It kind of broke my heart when I saw it," Streep continued. "I still can't get it out of my head because it wasn't in a movie. It was real life."
Streep said Trump's actions give permission to other people to do the same. "Disrespect invites disrespect. Violence incites violence. And when the powerful use their position to bully others, we all lose."
