
A 16-year-old singer, Nahid Afrin from Assam, who has already made a mark in Bollywood, is now fighting a fatwa issued by ulemas. As many as 46 clerics have asked her to stop performing as women performing in public is contrary to Islamic tradition.
The fatwa comes days ahead of a musical show to be held in Udali Sonai Bibi College in Nagaon district on March 25 where Nahid has been invited to perform. 
Since the venue of the event is in the vicinity of a mosque and a graveyard, the clerics have called for a boycott of her musical performance.
The young singer was shocked when she first heard of the fatwa against her. 
“I was shocked and devastated at first. But, many Muslim singers gave me inspiration to not quit music. I will never do so,” she said.
“I think my music is God’s gift to me. I believe that it must be properly utilised; not doing so is ignoring God,” she added.
According to reports, pamphlets bearing the names of the 46 clerics were found in different parts of Assam such as Lanka, Hojai areas where she is supposed to take part in a musical show. 
“If anti-Sharia acts like musical nights are held on grounds surrounded by masjids, idgahs, madrassas and graveyards, our future generations will attract the wrath of Allah,” the pamphlet read.
“Magic, dance, drama, theatre etc. are against Sharia laws. Events like musical shows are against the Sharia law and future generations will be corrupted,” it read.
Moreover, Muslim cleric Imam Umer Ilyasi said such steps only promote misunderstandings and that if someone is doing something against the Shariat then she is to be spoken personally.
“Even though Sharia does not allow singing and dancing, the ulema should think their approach towards the issue. If someone is doing something that is against the Sharia, declaring fatwas only complicate the issue and lead to misunderstanding,” Ilyasi was reported as saying.
Assam Chief Minister Sarbananda Sonowal strongly condemned the restrictions being put on Nahid’s performance and assured her security. “Freedom of artists are essence of democracy. Spoke to Nahid and reiterated our Govt’s commitment to provide safety and security to artists,” he tweeted. 
The Assam police are reportedly investigating whether the fatwa was a reaction to Nahid recently performing songs against terrorism, including the banned terror group Islamic State.
Earlier this month, a 22-year-old Muslim woman Suhana Sayed from Karnataka was trolled and abused for singing in the Kannada version of Sa Re Ga Ma Pa , where she sang a song in the praise of Lord Balaji in her audition.
A Facebook group called ‘Mangalore Muslims’ accused her of tarnishing the community’s image. 
The fundamentalists on their page belittled Suhana’s achievement saying that she has not achieved a great feat by winning appreciation from judges by singing a song in front of other men belonging to other religion.
