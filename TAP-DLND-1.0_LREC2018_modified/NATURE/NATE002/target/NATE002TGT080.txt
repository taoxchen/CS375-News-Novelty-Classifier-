 At least 21 people met their watery grave in the Ganga as two “overloaded” boats capsized opposite the Law College ghat in Patna on Saturday evening. 

One of the victims was identified as Bipul Kumar, a first-year engineering student of the National Institute of Technology, also situated on the Ganga bank nearby. He belonged to Mithapur village near Bihta in Patna district. The other deceased identified till late evening included Phutki (2) of Laheriasarai, Aditya Raj (24) of Gopalpur, Ladu (4) of Mahendru, Sonu Kumar (24) of Buxar, Bhulan Prajapati (26) of Deoria in UP, Raja Kumar (16) of Mata Khudi Lane in Mahendru, Rupa Devi (18) of Gulbi Ghat in Patna, Shanti Devi (62) of Mahendru, Priyanath Murmu (17) of Dumka in Jharkhand and Abhishek Srivastava (24) of Chowk Shikarpur in Patna City. 

Principal secretary (disaster management) Pratyaya Amrit told TOI around 11pm that 21 bodies had been recovered even as NDRF and SDRF teams were still engaged in search operations. Pratyaya said two countrymade motorboats collided a few metres from the Ganga diara side and capsized. One boat which could not be traced had at least 40 people on board, he said. Another boat was recovered late evening, he added. 

However, eyewitnesses said people on one boat started jumping on to another as water had started sipping inside it. In panic, both the boats lost balance and capsized. 

Patna DM Sanjay Kumar Agarwal said the Rs4 lakh ex gratia payment was made to the next of kin of each deceased at the mortuary late Saturday night. 

According to survivors, the boats were carrying at least 70 Makar Sankranti revellers who were on way back home after celebrating the festival in the diara area across the river. Thousands of people had sailed to the sandbar where the Bihar State Tourism Development Corporation (BSTDC) had organized a kite festival on the occasion. 

A private dolphin amusement park was also being run in the area without licence. While the state government has ordered an FIR against the park owner, police were also looking for Ashok Rai, owner of one of the ill-fated boats. He was said to be absconding. 

Police said the tragedy took place around 5.30pm just 10 metres away from the diara bank. At least 12 people were rescued by the staff of a BSTDC steamer, MV Kautilya Vihar, which was sailing nearby. 

“We threw 15 life jackets and air-filled tubes into the water, but the drowning people failed to catch them out of panic. Meanwhile, our colleague, Ajay Shankar Chaudhary, jumped into the river and saved few of them,” recalled Chetan Kumar, one of the Kautilya Vihar employees. 

Mohd Konin Alam, who was on the ill-fated boat along with his friend Mohd Dilshad Alam, said people scrambled to board the boats when they reached the diara. “They was overloaded. We thought of getting down, but such was the chaos that we could not as Dilshad is handicapped,” said Konin whose friend was missing. 

Konin and Alam had earlier come to Patna from Araria to take the Staff Selection Commission examination scheduled to be held in the state capital on Sunday. 

A wailing Usha Devi of Mahendru locality in Patna was seen shouting at people to rescue her daughters, Anjali (4) and Arpita (1). Along with the two kids, Usha’s sister-in-law Aarti Devi, nieces Lado and Chhoti and mother Narmada Devi were missing in the waters. 

Wails of family members also rent the air in the Patna Medical College & Hospital where the bodies as well as the injured were brought. Lying on a stretcher, Manju Devi cried as she frantically inquired from the authorities about her husband Shankar Prasad and grandson Tolu who had accompanied her to the diara.
