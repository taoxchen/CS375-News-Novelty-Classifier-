Normal life remained disrupted in Shimla and several other places in Himachal and tourists and local people faced great hardship even as the state started limping back to normalcy after the key hill stations and tribal areas experienced heaviest snowfall of the season. In spite of the claims of authorities regarding restoration of roads and water and power supply, tourists faced traffic jams and blockades. A large number of tourists coming to Shimla returned from Shoghi, 15 km from here. Power supply was partially restored in some area of Shimla town last night but most of the localities in Shimla town remained without electricity for 40 hours. Supply could be restored in some places this evening. However, water supply was badly affected as there was little pumping due to power breakdown while telecommunication services were partially restored. Upper Shimla region, tribal areas of Lahaul and Spiti, Kinnaur, Pangi, Bharmaur and the areas beyond Manali remained cut off from the rest of the state for the second day, notwithstanding the claims of the department concerned that 70 road, including Shimla-Kalka and Kullu-Manali national Highways, have been thrown open to traffic. Jyoti and Aman from Ludhiana said it took them seven hours to travel from Solan to Shimla. As they reached Shimla late in the evening no hotel room was availble. “As many as 70 roads, including Shimla-Manali and Shimla- Kalka National Highways, were opened for traffic today, Engineer-in-Chief Public Works AK Kohli said. As many as 535 roads were closed due to heavy snowfall yesterday. “Vehicular traffic has also been restored to popular tourist places like Kufri and Fagu on the outskirts of Shimla and Dalhousie in Chamba district. JCB machines, dozers and front enloaders, besides a large number of manpower, were pressed into service to clear the snow from the roads”, he added. Bus services from Shimla to Chandigarh and to Delhi, Mandi, Bilaspur, Kangra and Una have been resumed while buses to upper Shimla region and interiors of Sirmaur district remained suspended. Essential items like milk, bread, vegetables and newspapers reached Shimla in the afternoon but the areas of upper Shimla beyond Theog (32km from here) were still cut off. A large number of tourist vehicles were stranded at several places between Solan and Shoghi due to heavy rush and slippery roads. Additional Deputy Commissioner (Shimla) Rakesh Prajapati said the roads have been cleared up to Theog. Kalka -Shimla road has been thrown open up to Tuti Kandi. However, as a large number of tourists made a beeline for Shimla after snowfall, huge traffic jams are witnessed between Solan and Shoghi and the police had a tough time controlling the rush. Meanwhile, power supply was badly hit in Kasauli, Dagshai and Solan areas since last night as the 33 KV substation developed some fault after a tree fell on the power lines near Barog, eight km from Solan. The fault has not being rectified yet, forcing the residents to reel under cold weather. State capital Shimla and tribal and high altitude areas continued to reel under intense cold wave. Key tourists resorts of Manali and Shimla recorded minimum temperatures at minus 1.2 and minus 0.4 degrees followed Solan 2 degrees and Bhuntar 2.5 degrees while Kalpa and Keylong in tribal Kinnaur and Lahaul and Spiti districts recorded a low of minus 3.8 degree and minus 2.7 degrees C. Chief Minister Virbhadra Singh has directed the PWD, IPH and the HPSEB authorities to restore the roads, power and water supply in the affected areas at the earliest.
